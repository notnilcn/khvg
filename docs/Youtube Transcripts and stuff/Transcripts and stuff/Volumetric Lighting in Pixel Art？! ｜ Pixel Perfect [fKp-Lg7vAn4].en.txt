Nice stuff. Idea for improvement: add another layer of very-slow, high threshold noise that represents "dustyness" of the air at a point. Sample that for how intense the god rays are.

I’d recommend looking into Fractal Noise. Basically, stack a few different scales and thresholds of Perlin Noise together for more detailed blobs

I like the clean look of the blur, but I think there is a lot more to be explored with the noisy pixelated look. Maybe some quantization on a gradient like the shadows, or having it be sharper/tighter threshold. Both are very pretty and fit the overall esthetic, nice work!

9:40 I suggest that instead of omnidirectional blur like Gaussian you try to go with the very low sample size and smear along the light ray with directional blur a.k.a. fake motion blur. It should leave light filaments separated with sharp edges but at the same time they will be smeared along the ray and should look smooth and realistic. I also imagine that this should be "cheap". I'm artist not a programmer so I hope it makes sense.

If we're talking modern techniques, AFAIK the leading technique is frustum-shaped volume with XY resolution that is even smaller than your low-res render target, calculate lighting in each 'voxel', storing that in a 3d texture, then in screen space march towards the camera through this volume, accumulating results with trilinear sampling. Refer to: "VOLUMETRIC FOG: UNIFIED COMPUTE SHADER-BASED SOLUTION TO ATMOSPHERIC SCATTERING" by Bartlomiej Wronski from Siggraph 2014. This is what Unreal also does.

I like the god rays to be blurred and not be pixellated. Makes the world feel more pixillated, for the light to not be.

Add steps (like cloud mask) to the Godray fade calculation after the smoothing step? I am hoping you can utilize the performance of smoothing while having the ability to keep and control those crisp edges on the light shafts. I also think it might be more in line with your art direction. Your steps do not need to be equally spaced, either. If you can add a step weighting system, you can control the spacing between your steps via some function, like a log transform. This will also give you the distinct opportunity to add many more parameters which give control over the appearance of the Godray edges on screen. I am unfamiliar with Unity, but it might be possible to control what I am describing via a float curve

Could you add another parameter to the volumetric blur to add stepping/banding? Allow the blur to soften the noise, and then filter the blur to create crisper edges. I find the crispness of billboarded lightrays appealing, so finding a way to achieve that through simulation rather than hand crafting is intriguing.

A way to go ahead: After bluring the beams of light, apply the same stepped banding effect and align the steps with the cloud steps.

Suggestion: Shadow and lighting aren't technically any different, in more realistic styles it's less of an issue because of how blurred shadows and smooth shading interact, but here it looks a bit weird when the clouds pour over the balls and the diffuse shader bands of the balls intersect with the shadows. I'm not doing a pixel art style, but just a cel style, and I've found that rather than having the diffuse banding and shadows work separately, having the shadows be a subtraction factor to the toon/diffuse shader gives a smoother look, with shadows basically just warping/intensifying the toon banding which makes the gradient cleaner. Though it also makes the exact shapes of the shadows less defined (because you only see them secondarily via their effect on the diffuse banding), so maybe this is better for a pixel style that wants those defined edges.
