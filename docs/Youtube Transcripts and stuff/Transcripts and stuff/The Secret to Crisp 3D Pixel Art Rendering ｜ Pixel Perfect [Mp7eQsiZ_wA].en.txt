9:49 Wouldn't LUT be much faster? It would be sensible for HDR to be converted as floats, but for limited RGB pallett...
Thanks for the question. I'm not an expert on LUTs but I agree that using a LUT here is more performant and if I were to ship a game with this I'd probably go with that method. Just so we're on the same page, the conversions are the same here but the difference is moving between pre-baked and realtime. What I currently do in fragment would be moved to a 3d texture baker to produce a quantised LUT cube. And so the reason I chose to do it this way is that as I was producing content, the real-time aspect has many benefits such as being able swap in palettes and modify their colors on the fly, which is very handy. But I do agree with your assessment, thanks for picking up on that.
LUTs are good for smooth continuous functions as the (tri) linear filtering produces a nice approximation to such a function.  However you've got a discretely valued function, so would need to use nearest filtering in the LUT.  However this will not be a good approximation and you may find artifacts. You may want to use a LUT for the RGB to LAB conversion though, this only needs to be calculated once!  Best of both worlds.
